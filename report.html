India to Import Record 2.5 Million Tons of Urea at Nearly Double the Price Paid Two Months Ago

India is set to import a record 2.5 million metric tons of urea in a single tender, signaling a dramatic tightening of global supply and pushing prices to multi-year highs — with downstream effects expected across the full fertilizer complex.

India's government-backed fertilizer procurement agency has finalized a tender for 2.5 million metric tons of granular urea — the largest single purchase ever recorded — at a price approaching $700/ton, nearly double the ~$360/ton paid in February. The purchases, covering roughly a quarter of India's annual imports, are set to tighten global supply and push prices higher across the entire fertilizer complex.

📌 Key Context
The record purchase comes amid reduced Russian and Chinese export availability, gas price spikes in Europe reducing European urea output, and seasonal pre-kharif procurement demand in India. Analysts warn the ripple effects will reach DAP, MAP, and ammonia markets within 4–6 weeks.

Global urea supply has been under pressure since Q4 2024, with China — traditionally the swing exporter — implementing informal export restrictions to secure domestic food security. Russia, another major supplier, continues to face logistical constraints due to ongoing sanctions. This structural tightening left India with limited sourcing options, forcing acceptance of significantly higher prices to secure the required volumes ahead of the critical planting season.

The tender attracted bids from suppliers across the Middle East, North Africa, and South Asia. Saudi Arabia and Egypt were among the confirmed suppliers, with Saudi Aramco's fertilizer arm and Egypt's Abu Qir Fertilizers each contributing significant tonnage. Delivery is expected between May and June 2025, aligning with India's Kharif sowing window.

Market participants are closely monitoring whether this Indian purchase will pull volumes away from other regional buyers including Brazil, Southeast Asia, and East Africa — all of whom face their own seasonal demand peaks in the coming months. With global urea inventory at multi-year lows, the pressure on prices is unlikely to ease before Q3 2025.

What to watch: Chinese export policy decisions expected in late May, ammonia contract rollovers in June, and India's post-tender domestic price pass-through which could affect crop-planting decisions in the following season.
